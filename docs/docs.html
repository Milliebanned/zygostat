How Zygostat works.

Everything the product does, in plain language: the investment thesis, the evidence behind it, how each number is produced, and how to check any of it yourself. No background in blockchains is assumed.

01What Zygostat is

Zygostat is a long-term investment thesis for NEAR, built so that anyone can re-run it. It asks whether NEAR is worth holding over the long term, answers with evidence, and states in advance the results that would prove the answer wrong.

It has three parts:

  • The thesis: a written argument (THESIS.md) with a claim, a verdict, the evidence for it, the evidence against it, and four conditions that would end it.
  • The evidence: data read directly from the NEAR blockchain and from public market data, saved in the repository so every number can be traced and rebuilt.
  • The product: this website. The landing page summarises the thesis, and the product (Launch) lets you explore every piece of evidence and run the main check yourself, live.

The name is Greek: zygos, the beam of a scale, and statos, standing. The mark is a symbolon: in ancient Greece a token was broken in two, and a claim was proven when the two halves matched. That is what Zygostat checks: what is owed on one side, what is held on the other.

02The thesis in one minute

NEAR's base layer is shrinking while NEAR Intents, its cross-chain swap and settlement service, is growing fast. Intents is now the part of NEAR that earns meaningful money for the token. So the long-term case for NEAR is a bet that Intents becomes a place where cross-chain money settles, and stays one.

Today 4 of 4 conditions hold. Intents handled $3.91B in 30 days (+39% on a year earlier), NEAR captures about $21.5M a year from it, and the contract holding users' money holds every token it owes.

The case is conditional. Usage is real and growing. Value capture is real but small: captured revenue covers 22.4% of the value of the new NEAR issued each year, so holders are still diluted. And the contract that holds users' funds failed once, on 1 October 2026, when it owed $3.83M more of one token than it held. Zygostat measures that risk directly and prices it.

03The four conditions

A price is not evidence. Instead of arguing from conviction, the thesis names four results that would end it. Every build of the project recomputes all four from raw data. A condition that fails is printed as Triggered; it is never explained away.

The thresholds were written on 13 November 2026, after a first look at the data. That means they describe the past today; from the next rebuild on, they are real tests.

Why these four

  • K1 Solvency: a settlement layer that can owe more than it holds is not worth building on. This is the condition only chain data can test.
  • K2 Usage: if people stop using Intents, nothing else matters.
  • K3 Value capture: usage is only worth something to a NEAR holder if part of the revenue reaches NEAR.
  • K4 Security cost: if the expected cost of the next loss eats most of that revenue, the growth is not worth owning.

04How the solvency check works

NEAR Intents keeps users' money in one smart contract, intents.near. That contract keeps its own ledger of who owns what. The tokens themselves live in other contracts, one per token. So for every token there are two numbers on the public blockchain that should always agree:

  1. Credits: what intents.near's ledger says its users own of a token.
  2. Holdings: what that token's own contract says intents.near actually holds.

If credits are larger than holdings, users are owed tokens that are not there. Zygostat calls the difference the shortfall. A shortfall counts as material when it is worth at least $100 and is at least 0.01% of that token's credits; smaller gaps are still recorded.

Both numbers can be read for any block in the chain's history, so the check can be run today and also backwards in time. Zygostat reads them from a public archive of NEAR's state, with no account or key. Two kinds of token are checked: ordinary NEAR tokens (NEP-141) and the multi-tokens of the HOT Omni bridge (NEP-245). Comparisons use exact whole numbers, so rounding cannot hide a gap.

The latest reading, at block 224,790,855 (2026-11-16 08:16 UTC): 196 tokens checked, $209,590,641 credited, $209,597,008 held, shortfall $0. The contract holds slightly more than it owes, which is normal: people sometimes send tokens to it directly.

05The October exploit

On 1 October 2026 NEAR Intents was exploited for about $3.8M. The official postmortem explains that the contract kept a record of deposits that had in fact been refunded, so its ledger showed more than it held. That is exactly the gap described above, and it can be read back from the chain.

Zygostat took a reading every 3,000 blocks (about every 30 minutes) from 28 September to 5 October, 338 readings in all. On the affected token, USDT(DEPRECATED) on BSC:

Largest shortfall, $3,834,778 (99.8% of credits unbacked)2026-10-01 01:57 UTC

The largest gap measured, $3,834,778, agrees with the official $3.8M. The contract was under-backed for between 67.3 and 68.2 hours, and backing returned at 2026-10-02 20:30 UTC, one minute before the official fix was deployed at 20:31 UTC. The postmortem also notes that a similar issue was reported in January and another on 7 October, after which the feature responsible was removed.

06A year of readings

To see whether this ever happened at other times, the same check runs at midnight UTC: weekly from 2025-11-16, daily from 20 September 2026. Each of these readings checks credits for every token, and holdings for every multi-token and for the largest ordinary tokens (together most of the value credited).

Across 441 readings and 17,630 individual token checks, apart from the October exploit, no reading shows a material shortfall. Since 2026-10-08, 0 of 41 readings show one.

08The risk model

The October loss happened and was recovered. The question for an investor is what an honest allowance for the next one costs. The model is one line:

expected cost a year = events a year x (share lost x custody x (1 - recovered) x paid from revenue + revenue lost to the pause)

Base case: $1.03M a year, 4.8% of captured revenue. One event costs about 1.1 months of captured revenue, and events would have to happen 10.8 times a year to consume all of it. The Risk model view in the product has sliders for every assumption.

10Check it yourself

Nothing here asks to be trusted. With Node.js 22 or later, and nothing else installed:

node --test                  # the unit tests, offline
node src/build.mjs --check   # every page and number matches the committed data
node src/verify.mjs          # each claim in the thesis, checked against the raw readings
node src/verify.mjs --live   # the solvency check again, now, against the live chain

The raw data is in the repository under data/raw/, and node src/build.mjs rebuilds every page, number and document from it with no network. The data also refreshes itself: every day an automated job takes new readings from the chain and the market sources, rebuilds every page and number, and publishes the result, so the verdict on this site moves forward on its own. If a condition fails on new data, it is published as Triggered.

You can also reproduce the worst reading of the exploit without any of this code: the Exploit view lists two curl commands and the two numbers they should return.

12What it does not measure

  • The NEAR side only. The check proves intents.near holds the NEAR-side tokens it owes. It does not prove that each bridged token is backed on its home chain.
  • Listed tokens only. 203 of the 231 assets on the Intents list have a token contract on NEAR and are checked. The other 28 cannot be checked this way. NFTs and unlisted tokens are not checked.
  • Confidential balances are not on the public ledger.
  • History is sampled. A gap that opened and closed between two readings would not appear.
  • Past dollar values are approximate. Token amounts are exact; dollar totals use one price per token, taken on 2026-11-16.
  • Market figures are quoted from DefiLlama and NearBlocks, not recomputed.
  • One event. The loss rate rests on a single exploit, so its range is wide. The model shows the range instead of hiding it.
  • No forecast. Nothing here predicts the price of NEAR. This is research, not financial advice.

IssuanceNew NEAR created each year to pay for network security.

14Questions

Is this saying NEAR is a good investment?

It says the case for NEAR currently holds on four stated conditions, and shows what would change that. It does not forecast a price and is not financial advice.

Could Zygostat be wrong about solvency?

The two numbers come straight from the chain, and the worst October reading can be reproduced with two commands. What it cannot see is listed under What it does not measure.

Why trust the market numbers?

You do not have to. They are quoted from named public sources, saved with the code, and the solvency numbers do not depend on them.

What happens if a condition fails?

On the next rebuild it is printed as Triggered, on this site and in the thesis, and the verdict changes with it.

Does the live check send my data anywhere?

No. Your browser reads public data from a public NEAR node. Nothing passes through a server of ours, and no wallet is needed.
