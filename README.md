# Zygostat

A long-term investment thesis for NEAR that anyone can re-run. It tests the case for NEAR on three things, from public data and NEAR chain state: is NEAR Intents used, does NEAR capture part of what Intents earns, and does the contract holding users' money actually hold it. Four conditions, written down in advance, decide whether the case stands, and every build recomputes them.

Built at NEAR HACKATHON. Track: Investment Thesis (`thesis`).

- **The thesis:** [`THESIS.md`](THESIS.md)
- **The product:** [`docs/index.html`](docs/index.html) is the landing page; its **Launch** button opens the live product, [`docs/app.html`](docs/app.html). Open either file in a browser; no server needed.
- **The docs:** [`docs/docs.html`](docs/docs.html) explains everything the product does in plain language, for readers with no blockchain background. It is linked from every page as **Docs**.
- **Every number, unrounded:** [`out/results.json`](out/results.json)

The name is Greek: *zygos* (the beam of a scale) and *statos* (standing). The mark is a *symbolon*: in ancient Greece a token was broken in two, and a claim was proven when the two halves matched. Here the halves are what users are owed and what the contract holds.

## What it is

NEAR's base layer is shrinking while NEAR Intents grows, and Intents is now the only part of NEAR that earns meaningful revenue for the token. So the long-term case for NEAR is a bet that Intents becomes a place where cross-chain money settles, and stays one.

Zygostat turns that bet into four testable conditions:

| | Condition | What would end the case |
|---|---|---|
| K1 | Solvency | Any material shortfall in `intents.near` after the October 2026 fix |
| K2 | Usage | Intents 30-day volume below the same 30 days a year earlier |
| K3 | Value capture | Captured revenue under 10% of the value of a year's new NEAR supply |
| K4 | Security cost | The expected cost of the next loss above 25% of captured revenue |

K2 and K3 come from public market series. K1 and K4 need evidence a price chart cannot give, so Zygostat reads it from NEAR mainnet: for every token Intents routes, at any block, it compares two numbers.

1. **Credits:** what the `intents.near` contract's own ledger says users own.
2. **Held:** what the token's contract says `intents.near` actually holds.

If credits exceed holdings, users are owed money that is not there. That is what happened on 1 October 2026, and this repository reproduces it, block by block, from archival state. The size of that event then prices K4: how much of NEAR's captured revenue an honest allowance for the next one would consume.

## The problem it solves

Most analysis of NEAR measures volume, fees and price, then argues from conviction. This thesis states in advance what would prove it wrong and checks it against raw data on every build, so a reader can verify the verdict instead of trusting it.

It also covers the one risk the usual numbers miss. The official postmortem of the October exploit says the team compared origin-chain balances with NEAR-side balances, but not users' credits with the contract's own holdings. That comparison needs no private data: it is two public view calls. Zygostat makes them for every listed token, across a year of blocks, and keeps the raw answers.

```sh
node src/verify.mjs
```

**See:** a `PASS` or `FAIL` line for every claim the thesis makes, then `ALL CHECKS PASS`. It uses only the committed raw readings. The claims checked:

- the outputs are a pure function of the data;
- every reading has a block height, a time, credits and holdings, and no failed request;
- at the latest reading no token has a material shortfall;
- the exploit-week readings contain a material shortfall, its largest value is within 5% of the $3.8M in the official postmortem, it starts after the first attacker transaction and before the pause, and backing returns within three days;
- no reading after 2026-10-08 has a material shortfall;
- total supply quoted from NearBlocks agrees with NEAR block headers to within 0.1%;
- each of the four conditions could be measured.

## How the solvency check works

`intents.near` is the contract that holds users' funds for NEAR Intents. It keeps an internal ledger of who owns what. Tokens arrive in it in two forms:

| Token form | Example | Credits read with | Holdings read with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fungible token (NEP-141) | `nep141:wrap.near`, `nep141:eth-0xa0b8….omft.near` | `intents.near :: mt_batch_supply` | `<token contract> :: ft_balance_of({account_id: "intents.near"})` |
| Multi token (NEP-245), used by the HOT Omni bridge | `nep245:v2_1.omni.hot.tg:56_…` | `intents.near :: mt_batch_supply` | `v2_1.omni.hot.tg :: mt_batch_balance_of({account_id: "intents.near", token_ids})` |

For each token: `gap = credits - held`.

- `gap <= 0`: fully backed. A small surplus is normal (tokens sent to the contract directly).
- `gap > 0`: a shortfall. Users are credited with tokens the contract does not hold.

A shortfall is **material** when it is worth at least $100 **and** is at least 1 basis point (0.01%) of that token's credits. Both thresholds are in [`src/metrics.mjs`](src/metrics.mjs). Smaller gaps are still recorded and reported.

Comparisons use exact integers (`BigInt`) in the token's smallest unit. Dollar values are for display and totals only.

### What was read

| Plan | When | What each reading covers | Requests each |
|---|---|---|---|
| `head` | The chain head at build time | Every checkable token | about 170 |
| `series` | UTC midnight: weekly for a year, daily from 2026-09-20 | Credits for every token; holdings for every multi token and for the largest fungible tokens (those covering most of the credited value; the list is in `data/raw/major.json`) | about 30 |
| `micro` | Every 3,000 blocks (about 30 minutes) from 2026-09-28 to 2026-10-05 | Credits and holdings for every multi token | 2 |
| `full` (optional) | Any UTC midnights you choose, e.g. monthly | Credits and holdings for every token, as at the head | about 170 |

`node src/scan.mjs majors` chooses the fungible tokens the `series` plan reads holdings for, from the head reading (the largest by credited value until 98% is covered, at least 8 and at most 24). Every plan skips readings already on disk, so an interrupted scan resumes where it stopped.

The public node's limit of about 60 requests a minute sets these choices. Each reading records how much of the credited value had its backing read (`checkedShare`).

## The risk model

`expected cost a year = events a year x (f x C x (1 - recovered) x h + D)`

| Symbol | Meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| R | Revenue NEAR captures from Intents in a year | DefiLlama, last 90 days annualised (quoted) |
| C | Custody: credits for every token, in dollars | The head reading (chain) |
| f | Share of custody lost in one event | Largest shortfall in October over custody that day (chain) |
| events a year | How often a loss event happens | One exploited loss over the life of Intents, with an exact 90% Poisson interval |
| recovered | Share of a loss that comes back | 100% was observed once. The base case uses 50%. |
| D | Revenue lost to the pause that follows an event | Fees over the pause days against the same weekdays a week earlier, times the capture share |
| h | Share of an unrecovered loss that captured revenue ends up paying | Assumption: 100%, the hardest case for holders |

Outputs: expected cost a year, its share of R, risk-adjusted R, the event rate that would consume all of R, and one event measured in months of R. `THESIS.md` shows a three-by-three grid over the event rate and the recovery rate. The dashboard has sliders for four of the inputs.

## What would change the thesis

Four conditions, recomputed on every build:

| | Condition |
|---|---|
| K1 | No reading after 2026-10-08 shows a material shortfall. |
| K2 | Intents 30-day volume is not below the same 30 days a year earlier. |
| K3 | Captured revenue (90 days annualised) is at least 10% of the value of a year's new NEAR supply. |
| K4 | Expected yearly security cost (observed rate, half recovered) is at most 25% of captured revenue. |

The thresholds were written on 2026-11-13, after a first look at the data. That makes them descriptions of the past today, and tests from the next rebuild on. `THESIS.md` says so in the same words.

## Live data

The repository refreshes itself every day. A scheduled GitHub Action (`.github/workflows/refresh.yml`, 01:30 UTC) reads the token list and market series again, takes a new solvency reading of every token at the chain head, adds the daily readings for the last seven days, rebuilds every page and number, runs the tests, and commits the result. A second workflow (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`) publishes `docs/` to GitHub Pages after every push and every refresh.

So the dashboards, `THESIS.md`, the README results table and the four conditions move forward on their own. If a condition is triggered by new data, the refresh still publishes it: the thesis says TRIGGERED, and the verify step in the same run reports which check failed. The refresh can also be started by hand from the repository's Actions tab (`refresh`, then `Run workflow`).

## Data sources

All keyless. All committed under `data/raw/`, so every step above except the live ones works with no network.

| Source | Used for | Endpoint | Committed as |
|---|---|---|---|
| NEAR mainnet state, public archival RPC | Credits and holdings at any block | `https://archival-rpc.mainnet.fastnear.com` (JSON-RPC `query` / `call_function`) | `data/raw/scan/**` |
| NEAR block headers | Block time and total supply | `https://mainnet.neardata.xyz/v0/block/<height>/headers`, or RPC `block` | inside each reading; `data/raw/blocks.json` |
| NEAR Intents 1Click API | The token list: asset ids, decimals, a price | `https://1click.chaindefuser.com/v0/tokens` | `data/raw/registry.json` |
| DefiLlama | Intents volume, fees, revenue captured by NEAR; NEAR gas fees | `https://api.llama.fi/summary/dexs/near-intents`, `.../summary/fees/near-intents?dataType=dailyFees`, `...dailyRevenue`, `.../summary/fees/near?dataType=dailyFees` | `data/raw/series.json` |
| NearBlocks | NEAR price, total supply, transactions, active accounts | `https://api.nearblocks.io/v1/charts` | `data/raw/series.json` |
| Official postmortem; CertiK analysis | The reported timeline of the exploit (quoted, linked in `THESIS.md` and the dashboard) | see [`src/results.mjs`](src/results.mjs) `INCIDENT` | in code |

`data/raw/series.json` stores the SHA-256 of each response as served.

## NEAR's role

NEAR is the subject and the data source.

- Every solvency number is a view call against NEAR mainnet contracts (`intents.near`, the token contracts, `v2_1.omni.hot.tg`) at a specific block.
- The check exists because of how NEAR works: its asynchronous cross-contract calls are what let a deposit be credited while its tokens were refunded. The official postmortem says exactly this.
- The dashboard's live check talks to NEAR RPC from the browser.
- No contract is deployed and no transaction is signed. Nothing here needs a wallet or funds.

There are no mandatory integrations in this hackathon (rule Q-011). This project reads NEAR; it does not write to it.

## Limits

- **NEAR side only.** It proves `intents.near` holds the NEAR-side tokens it credits. It does not prove a bridged token is backed on its origin chain.
- **Listed tokens only.** Tokens on the 1Click list with a NEAR token contract are checked. Listed assets with no NEAR-side token cannot be. Tokens in the contract that are not on the list, and NFTs, are not checked.
- **Confidential balances** are not on the public ledger and are out of scope.
- **History is sampled.** A shortfall that opened and closed between two readings would not appear. The series plan reads holdings for the largest fungible tokens, not all of them.
- **Dollar values for past dates are approximate.** Token amounts are exact; one price per token is used, taken when the registry was fetched.
- **Third-party series are quoted.** Volume, fees, revenue, price and activity come from DefiLlama and NearBlocks and are not recomputed. Supply is cross-checked against block headers.
- **The event rate rests on one event.** The model shows the width of the interval instead of hiding it.
- **Public node limits.** Refreshing the full history from the public node takes over an hour. Verifying it does not: the readings are committed.
- **Not done:** an on-chain record of the readings; alerts; origin-chain checks.
